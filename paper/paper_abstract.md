# Proactive Kubernetes Autoscaling Using Patch-Based Transformer Models

## Abstract (IC2E 2026)

Kubernetes Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA) relies on reactive threshold-based scaling, which introduces significant latency during sudden traffic surges in microservice architectures. This paper presents a proactive autoscaling framework that leverages Transformer-based time-series forecasting to predict resource demand and pre-provision pods before traffic spikes occur. We deploy a real-world Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE) cluster running the Google Online Boutique microservice application and collect a high-fidelity dataset of 28,108 time-aligned observations comprising CPU utilization, memory consumption, request throughput (QPS), and P95 latency from Prometheus monitoring. We evaluate two deep learning architectures—a vanilla Transformer encoder and PatchTST (Patch Time Series Transformer)—against the standard Kubernetes HPA baseline. Our experiments demonstrate that the vanilla Transformer achieves an 89.3% reduction in prediction MSE over reactive HPA, while PatchTST further improves prediction stability with a 92.0% reduction in MAE. In live cluster benchmarks conducted over five independent runs, the proactive AI controller reduces P95 request latency from 1,020 ms to 215 ms—a 79% improvement—by initiating scaling decisions 45–60 seconds ahead of traffic surges. Statistical significance is confirmed across all runs. These results establish that Transformer-based proactive autoscaling substantially outperforms reactive HPA in latency-sensitive microservice deployments, with PatchTST offering superior robustness for production workloads through its patch-wise attention mechanism.

**Keywords**: Kubernetes, Autoscaling, Transformer, PatchTST, Time-Series Forecasting, Cloud Engineering, Microservices
